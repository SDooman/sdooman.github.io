The sign of the magnitude of a size transformation tells whether the pre-image and image are on the same side (positive magnitude) or opposite side (negative magnitude) of the center. True conjunctive statements

Three statements can be extracted from the string of symbols

“3=9 and −22=43=\sqrt{9}\text{ and }{}^{-}2^{2}=43=9​ and −22=4.”

These three statements are:

#1: 3=93=\sqrt{9}3=9​ #2: −22=4{}^{-}2^{2}=4−22=4 #3: 3=9 and −22=43=\sqrt{9}\text{ and }{}^{-}2^{2}=43=9​ and −22=4

Statement #3, formed by placing the word “and” between statements #1 and #2 is called a “conjunctive” statement. Statement #1 is true and statement #2 is false. Statement #3 is also false. It is “contaminated” by the presence of statement #2 within it.

In general, when a conjunctive statement is formed by writing “and” between two other statements, that conjunctive statement is true only if both of the statements from which it is formed are true.

The open sentences “x2=4x^{2}=4x2=4” and “x<0x<0x<0” can be used to form the conjunctive open sentence

is not true. It is contaminated by “(¯3)2 = 4.”

However, ¯2 is a solution because

“(¯2)2 = 4 and ¯2 < 0”

is true.

The conjunctive open sentence
